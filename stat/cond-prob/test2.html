Test 2: Problems with numbers

15 problems in mixed order. For each one: name the events A and B, state the type and say why, then apply the rule. Give answers as decimals (or fractions where stated).

Mutually exclusive: P(A ∩ B) = 0. Independent: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B). Dependent: use the overlap from the data. Always: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).

Problem 1

Toss a coin and roll a die. A = Heads. B = a 6. Find P(A ∩ B) as a fraction and as a decimal (4 places).

Independent. 1/12 ≈ 0.0833

Problem 2

A student gets one final grade. Event X: the grade is A (chance 0.15). Event Y: the grade is B (chance 0.35). Find (a) P(X ∩ Y) and (b) P(X ∪ Y).

Mutually exclusive. (a) 0 (b) 0.50

Problem 3

Over 500 days, 150 were cloudy, 100 had rain, and 90 were both cloudy and rainy. Find (a) P(cloudy ∩ rain) and (b) P(cloudy ∪ rain). (c) Is multiplying 0.30 × 0.20 correct for AND here?

Dependent. (a) 0.18 (b) 0.32 (c) No: 0.06 is not 0.18.

Problem 4

200 students. 80 play a sport, 60 are left-handed, and 24 do both. (a) Find P(sport ∩ left-handed) from the data. (b) Compare it with 0.40 × 0.30. Are the two events independent or dependent?

Independent. (a) 0.12 (b) 0.40 × 0.30 = 0.12, the same as the data, so the events are independent.

Problem 5

A flight’s status is recorded at departure time. The chance it is on time is 0.80 and the chance it is cancelled is 0.10. Find (a) P(On time ∩ Cancelled) and (b) P(On time ∪ Cancelled).

Mutually exclusive. (a) 0 (b) 0.90

Problem 6

A factory in Dhaka meets its monthly target with chance 0.20 (A). A factory owned by a different company in Vietnam meets its monthly target with chance 0.10 (B). Find (a) P(A ∩ B) and (b) P(A ∪ B).

Independent. (a) 0.02 (b) 0.28

Problem 7

A bag has 5 red and 5 blue marbles. Draw one marble, keep it out, then draw a second. There are 90 equally likely ordered pairs, and 20 of them are red then red. (a) Find P(both red) as a fraction. (b) Compare with 0.5 × 0.5. Are the two draws independent?

Dependent. (a) 20/90 = 2/9 ≈ 0.2222 (b) 0.25 is not 0.2222, so the draws are dependent.

Problem 8

Pick one month of the year at random. A = the month is January, February or March. B = the month is July, August or September. Find (a) P(A ∩ B) and (b) P(A ∪ B).

Mutually exclusive. (a) 0 (b) 6/12 = 0.50

Problem 9

Toss a coin twice. A = Heads on toss 1. B = Heads on toss 2. Find P(A ∩ B).

Independent. 0.25

Problem 10

300 employees. 120 work in sales, 90 get a bonus, and 72 are in sales and get a bonus. Find (a) P(sales ∩ bonus) and (b) P(sales ∪ bonus). (c) Among the sales staff only, what fraction get a bonus? Compare it with the fraction of all employees who get a bonus. Does knowing someone is in sales change the chance of a bonus?

Dependent. (a) 0.24 (b) 0.46 (c) 72/120 = 0.60 in sales, against 90/300 = 0.30 overall. Yes, it changes the chance, so the events are dependent.

Problem 11

Roll a die once. A = you roll a 5. B = you roll a 6. Find (a) P(A ∩ B) and (b) P(A ∪ B) as a fraction.

Mutually exclusive. (a) 0 (b) 1/3

Problem 12

In a bookshop, A = a customer’s receipt number is even (chance 0.50) and B = the customer buys a novel (chance 0.30). Find (a) P(A ∩ B), (b) P(A ∪ B), and (c) out of 200 customers, about how many have an even receipt number AND buy a novel?

Independent. (a) 0.15 (b) 0.65 (c) 30

Problem 13

On 100 mornings: 18 had heavy traffic only, 42 had heavy traffic and a late arrival, 10 had a late arrival only, and 30 had neither. Find (a) P(traffic ∩ late), (b) P(traffic ∪ late), and (c) say whether the two events are independent, using P(traffic) × P(late).

Dependent. (a) 0.42 (b) 0.70 (c) 0.60 × 0.52 = 0.312, not 0.42, so dependent.

Problem 14

A parcel goes through two checks run by different staff on different machines. A = it passes the weight check (0.90). B = it passes the label check (0.80). Find (a) P(A ∩ B) and (b) P(A ∪ B).

Independent. (a) 0.72 (b) 0.98

Problem 15

Toss a coin once. A = Heads. B = Tails. Find (a) P(A ∩ B), (b) P(A ∪ B), and (c) say whether A and B are independent or dependent, and why.

Mutually exclusive. (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) Dependent. Heads and Tails cannot happen together, so one rules out the other.
