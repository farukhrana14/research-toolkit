Pearson Correlation Test

How do I get r in Excel or Google Sheets?

Pearson: type =CORREL(A2:A31, B2:B31). Change the ranges to your two columns.

Spearman: first rank each column. In a new column, type =RANK.AVG(A2, $A$2:$A$31, 1) and copy it down. Do the same for the second column. Then use =CORREL on the two rank columns.

Tip: copy the full value that CORREL gives you (for example 0.48231). Do not round it first, because rounding can change the p-value a little.

Dancey and Reidy's published bands leave small gaps (for example, between 0.3 and 0.4). This tool smooths out those edges so every value gets a label. That is why it says "adapted from".

Important classroom notes

Choose the alternative hypothesis (H₁) before you look at the result.

Caution: the three decision lines above are three different hypothesis decisions, not three things that are true at the same time.

H₀ (null hypothesis): ρ = 0. This means there is no relation between the two variables in the population.

Formula: t = r × √(n − 2) ÷ √(1 − r²), with df = n − 2. The p-value comes from Student's t-distribution.

Spearman: this tool uses the same t formula for Spearman's ρ. It is accurate when n is about 10 or more.

Say "fail to reject H₀", not "accept H₀". Failing to reject means the data do not give enough evidence. It does not prove that there is no relation.
